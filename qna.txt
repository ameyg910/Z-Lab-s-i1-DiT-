1. Why does RMSNorm cast to fp32 inside the function, and why cast back at the end? What would go wrong
   if you computed x.square().mean() directly in bf16?
Ans. bf16 is a compressed number format that saves memory, but it only tracks 2 to 3 significant digits. 
     fp32 tracks about 7 significant digits. 
     To calculate the mean the code squares hundreds or thousands of numbers and adds them all up one by one. 
     As that running sum grows larger, bf16 starts ignoring the smaller numbers being added to the pile.
     Because those small numbers get dropped, the final average comes out slightly too low.
     Because you are dividing by the square root, a small error in the average gets magnified.
     Every word (token) passing through the model ends up scaled slightly wrong, 
     and this tiny mistake repeats in every single layer.
     Converting the numbers to fp32 just for this addition step solves the problem completely.
     Why cast back at the end?
     The rest of the network runs in bf16 for speed and memory. 
     Handing back the original dtype keeps the next matmul and the residual stream in the precision they expect. 
     The norm is the only place that needs fp32, and it's cheap: just elementwise ops on one tensor.


2. Prove the 2/3 factor. A GELU MLP with hidden size 4D has about how many weights, 
   in terms of D? A SwiGLU with hidden size h has about how many? Solve for h.
Ans. A GELU MLP is D->4D and 4D->D. so it has 4D^2 + 4D^2 = 8D^2 weights. 
     A SwiGLU with hidden size h has three matrices (W1, W2, W3), each D*h, so 3Dh weights.
     Setting them up equal results 3Dh = 8D^2 => h = (2/3)*4D.

3. For our mini config (D = 512), what is hidden exactly? (Watch the two int() calls.)
Ans. int(512 * 4) = 2048, then 2/3 * 2048 = 1365.33, and int() truncates that to 1365. 
     So w12 is Linear(512, 2730) and w3 is Linear(1365, 512).

4. Shapes check. At 512px, how many image tokens are there, and what's the total sequence length once you add the text tokens?
Ans. Going from 512 to 64 is the VAE's /8. Going from 64 to 32 is the 2*2 patching. 
     So there are 32 * 32 = 1024 image tokens. Adding 256 text tokens gives 1280 tokens total. 
     That's 2.5 times the length at 256px, and since attention cost grows with length squared, 
     attention gets about 6 times more expensive.

1. Parameter count of x_embedder for the mini config: weights plus bias. 
   Show the arithmetic. Then explain in one or two sentences why it equals a Linear(128, 512).
Ans. weight: out_ch * in_ch * k * k = 512 * 32 * 2 * 2 = 65,536
     bias:   512
     total:  66,048
     Why that equals Linear(128, 512): each patch is 32 * 2 * 2 = 128 numbers. 
     The windows don't overlap, and every patch is multiplied by the same weights. 
     A Linear(128, 512) has 128 * 512 + 512 = 66,048 parameters, the exact same count.
